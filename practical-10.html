Practical 10 of 12 · Synthetic media

Looks real. Is it authentic?

An image that looks real may be synthetic; a synthetic image may look entirely ordinary.

◷ 7 minutesIntermediate7 step checklistFree tools only
Session tips ↗

01 / Learning objective

Use source metadata and reverse-image discovery rather than unreliable visual guesses.

02 / Follow the procedure

Check each step as you teach.

03 / Exact prompts

Click Copy exact prompt, open the linked free chat tool, paste and run. Each response can vary.

✦ Optional · Generate a synthetic scene
Generate a photorealistic picture of a FICTIONAL university library interior, including bookshelves, desks, daylight and students studying. Use a realistic documentary style. Do not depict a named real university or real identifiable person. This is for an AI-media literacy class; label it AI-generated when displaying it.

04 / Observe & discuss

WHAT YOU MIGHT SEE

Participants may misclassify visuals; Commons pages give documented provenance for chosen samples.

ASK THE AUDIENCE

Can a reverse-image-search result by itself prove that an image is authentic?

ETHICAL / TECHNICAL SAFEGUARD

Always inspect the actual Commons file page; category pages contain many different works and licenses. Do not assume an image is free of attribution requirements.

05 / Instructor answer & takeaway

Keep the answer hidden while participants predict or compare results.

File provenance, original creator details and context provide stronger evidence than visual guessing. Google Lens may find matches, but no reverse-image search can conclusively establish that an image is real or AI-generated.
Learning checkpoint: I verified file-page provenance and did not equate Lens results with an AI-detection verdict.
