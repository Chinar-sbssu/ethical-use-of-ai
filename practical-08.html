Confidence is not a calculator

Sometimes the right answer is less important than being able to check it.

◷ 5 minutesBeginner6 step checklistFree tools only
Session tips ↗

01 / Learning objective

Demonstrate independent verification of arithmetic and intuitive reasoning puzzles.

02 / Follow the procedure

Check each step as you teach.

03 / Exact prompts

Click Copy exact prompt, open the linked free chat tool, paste and run. Each response can vary.

✦ A · Percentage trap
A product costs ₹1,000. Its price increases by 20% and then decreases by 20%. What is the final price? Show the arithmetic and verify the answer by working backward.
✦ B · Bat-and-ball puzzle
A bat and a ball together cost ₹110. The bat costs ₹100 more than the ball. What does the ball cost? Set up an equation, solve it, and substitute the values back to verify.

04 / Observe & discuss

WHAT YOU MIGHT SEE

Modern AI systems may solve both correctly. This tests verification habits rather than guaranteeing a model failure.

ASK THE AUDIENCE

What independent check would you use for a more complex numerical claim?

ETHICAL / TECHNICAL SAFEGUARD

Use independent methods for consequential finances, grades, engineering and medicine; do not treat chain-of-thought-looking prose as proof.

05 / Instructor answer & takeaway

Keep the answer hidden while participants predict or compare results.

A: ₹1,000 × 1.20 × 0.80 = ₹960. B: If ball = x, bat = x + 100. Then 2x + 100 = 110, so x = ₹5 (bat = ₹105).
Learning checkpoint: I independently verified both answers rather than relying on the chatbot.
