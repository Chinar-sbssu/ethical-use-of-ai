Practical 03 of 12 · Plagiarism

Paraphrased ≠ original idea

Different words do not automatically mean different intellectual ownership.

◷ 6 minutesBeginner6 step checklistFree tools only
Session tips ↗

01 / Learning objective

Show the distinction between paraphrasing, acknowledging an idea and fabricating a citation.

02 / Follow the procedure

Check each step as you teach.

Prepared classroom example

Use this fixed sample when consistency matters.

SIMULATED CLASSROOM HANDOUT — NOT A REAL PUBLICATION
Author: R. Classroom (2026)
Title: Learning with AI: A teaching note

“AI tools can help learners by supplying explanations, practice tasks and feedback. Depending on how they are used, such tools may also reduce opportunities to practice independent reasoning and critical evaluation.”

03 / Exact prompts

Click Copy exact prompt, open the linked free chat tool, paste and run. Each response can vary.

✦ A · Rewrite the provided passage
Paraphrase this classroom passage clearly without changing its meaning. Then list the ideas that still came from the original author: “AI tools can help learners by supplying explanations, practice tasks and feedback. Depending on how they are used, such tools may also reduce opportunities to practice independent reasoning and critical evaluation.”
✦ B · Make attribution explicit
This is a SIMULATED source for teaching only: R. Classroom (2026), “Learning with AI: A teaching note.” Show one example of a paraphrase with an in-text attribution for a fictional classroom exercise. Clearly warn me not to cite this invented source in real academic work.

04 / Observe & discuss

WHAT YOU MIGHT SEE

Paraphrases should preserve meaning. The original intellectual contribution still belongs to its source.

ASK THE AUDIENCE

When is a paraphrase still academic misconduct?

ETHICAL / TECHNICAL SAFEGUARD

Do not confuse similarity scores with proof of plagiarism. This example source is intentionally fictional.

05 / Instructor answer & takeaway

Keep the answer hidden while participants predict or compare results.

If someone else originated the idea, merely changing wording does not remove the need for citation. Citation must identify a real source when submitted as actual research.
Learning checkpoint: I can distinguish a paraphrase with correct attribution from unattributed borrowing.
