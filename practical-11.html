Practical 11 of 12 · Research integrity

Research without fabricated evidence

An AI-generated research summary must survive a trip to the actual papers.

◷ 7 minutesIntermediate7 step checklistFree tools only
Session tips ↗

01 / Learning objective

Move from research claims to search terms, real papers, methods and limitations.

02 / Follow the procedure

Check each step as you teach.

03 / Exact prompts

Click Copy exact prompt, open the linked free chat tool, paste and run. Each response can vary.

✦ A · Plan the evidence search
I am researching links between social media use and academic outcomes among university students. Give three testable hypotheses, five keywords for Google Scholar, three plausible confounders and five factual claims that would require primary-source evidence. Distinguish correlation from causation. Do not invent citations.
✦ B · Critically appraise what I supply
I will paste a paper abstract or my own notes. Based ONLY on that text, identify: (1) research question, (2) method, (3) sample, (4) finding, (5) limitations, (6) possible confounders, and (7) whether any causal conclusion is supported. Explicitly mark every detail as “not provided” when absent. Do not invent author or citation details.

04 / Observe & discuss

WHAT YOU MIGHT SEE

AI can organize a review, but independent examination of the real source may reveal caveats or differences.

ASK THE AUDIENCE

What detail from the original study changed your interpretation of the AI summary?

ETHICAL / TECHNICAL SAFEGUARD

Some papers are paywalled. Reading a title or abstract is not the same as reading the entire paper; do not overstate the evidence.

05 / Instructor answer & takeaway

Keep the answer hidden while participants predict or compare results.

Responsible research uses AI for support—question framing, search terms and critique—but supports claims using traceable literature and its actual methods.
Learning checkpoint: I checked a real source and documented at least one methodological limitation.
